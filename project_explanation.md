## 1. 30-Second Elevator Pitch

> *"Our project is an end-to-end Big Data analytics and machine learning pipeline for detecting fake spam reviews on e-commerce platforms like Amazon. Because consumer purchase decisions depend directly on customer reviews, malicious sellers flood products with fake 5-star ratings or post fake 1-star attack reviews on competitors. With over 232 million reviews totaling 140+ GB of data, single-machine tools like Pandas crash with Out-Of-Memory errors. We built a distributed system using Hadoop HDFS for reliable block storage, MapReduce and Scala for distributed data cleansing, Apache Spark for in-memory DAG processing, and Spark MLlib to train a multimodal NLP spam classifier that achieves 88.64% accuracy."*

## 2. Problem Statement & Why Big Data

### The Problem
* **Review Manipulation**: Up to 30% of reviews in popular categories (electronics, beauty, home) are generated by review farms, bots, or incentivized reviewers.
* **Loss of Trust**: Consumers buy low-quality or dangerous products based on artificially inflated 5-star ratings.
* **Market Sabotage**: Competitors launch smear campaigns by posting coordinated 1-star negative attacks.

### Why Standard Data Science Tools Fail (The Big Data Justification)
| Tool | What Happens on 140 GB Dataset | Why It Fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Python / Pandas** | Immediate `MemoryError` | Pandas requires loading the entire dataset into RAM at once ($\sim 2.5\times$ file size $\approx 350\text{ GB RAM}$ needed). |
| **MySQL / PostgreSQL** | Storage exhaustion & query timeouts | Relational databases lock tables on ingestion and cannot parallelize queries across disk partitions. |
| **Scikit-Learn** | Process killed by OS (`OOMKilled`) | Training algorithms in Scikit-Learn are single-threaded or single-node; cannot scale beyond a single CPU's memory. |
| **Apache Spark + HDFS (Our Choice)** | Executes in parallel in minutes | Dataset is split across distributed 128 MB blocks; computation is scheduled across multiple CPU cores in parallel via Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAG). |

## 3. Dataset Overview & The 5 V's of Big Data

* **Source**: Amazon Customer Reviews Dataset (EMNLP Research Benchmark by Julian McAuley, UCSD).
* **Raw Compressed Size**: 33.5 GB (`.json.gz`).
* **Raw Uncompressed Size**: 140+ GB of line-delimited JSON.
* **Total Records**: ~232,800,000 (232.8 Million) reviews spanning 29 product categories.
* **Storage Optimization**: Converted to **49.8 GB Apache Parquet** with Snappy compression (64.4% disk reduction).

### The 5 V's of Big Data in This Project (Classic 3 V's + Modern 5 V's)
> **Note on 3 V's vs 5 V's**: The syllabus guidelines specifically prompted for *"volume / variety / velocity"* (the classic 3 V's definition). However, our project completely addresses all **5 V's of Big Data**, with **Veracity and Value** representing the core purpose of spam detection:

1. **VOLUME (Scale of Data)**:
   * 232.8 Million rows of text and metadata ($\sim 140\text{ GB}$ uncompressed).
   * 161 GB of active replicated storage across 2 Hadoop DataNodes.
2. **VARIETY (Diversity of Formats)**:
   * Semi-structured JSON lines (`All_Amazon_Review.json`).
   * Unstructured natural language review text (`reviewText`, `summary`).
   * Structured columnar binary format (`.parquet`).
   * Aggregated tabular reports (`.csv`).
3. **VELOCITY (Speed of Ingestion & Processing)**:
   * Zero-shuffle streaming into Parquet without disk spilling.
   * High-throughput in-memory DAG analytics and distributed batch queries in Apache Spark.
4. **VERACITY (Trustworthiness & Quality of Data — The Core Problem)**:
   * E-commerce reviews are tainted with deceitful reviews, bot spam, unverified buyers, and rating manipulation.
   * Our project directly tackles Veracity by filtering noise, identifying spam patterns, and establishing data integrity.
5. **VALUE (Business Impact & Decision Making)**:
   * Restores consumer trust in online shopping.
   * Protects honest sellers from malicious 1-star competitor attacks.
   * Prevents buyers from wasting money on fraudulent or dangerous products.

### Key Attributes Used in Spam Detection
| Column Name | Data Type | Role in Spam Detection |
|---|---|---|
| `verified` | Boolean (`true`/`false`) | **Strongest behavioral signal.** `true` means Amazon confirmed the buyer purchased the item. `false` indicates high-risk unverified reviews. |
| `reviewText` | String | Body of the review analyzed by Spark NLP for spam vocabulary and bot repetition. |
| `overall` | Double (`1.0` to `5.0`) | Star rating. Spam reviews heavily skew towards extreme ratings (5.0 or 1.0), avoiding neutral 3.0. |
| `helpful` | Array[Int] (`[upvotes, total]`) | Real reviews accumulate upvotes over time; spam reviews almost always receive `0` helpful votes. |
| `asin` | String | Amazon Standard Identification Number (Product ID). Identifies products under review bombing campaigns. |
| `reviewTime` | String | Review submission date. Used to detect sudden temporal bursts in review volume. |

## 5. End-to-End Pipeline Walkthrough

### Phase 1: Distributed Ingestion to HDFS
* Raw data was loaded into HDFS at `/project/input/`.
* The NameNode splits incoming files into **128 MB blocks** and distributes them across `datanode1` and `datanode2`.
* Replication factor is set to 2 so every block exists on both DataNodes for fault tolerance.

### Phase 2: MapReduce Processing (Java)
* To satisfy the traditional distributed computing paradigm, a Hadoop MapReduce job was written in Java:
  * **Mapper (`ReviewRatingMapper.java`)**: Reads raw JSON lines, parses the `overall` star rating, and emits `(Rating, 1)`.
  * **Reducer (`ReviewRatingReducer.java`)**: Aggregates counts for each rating score and outputs distribution totals.

### Phase 3: Scala Functional Preprocessing
* Apache Spark is written in Scala. We implemented `AmazonPreprocessing.scala` to leverage native JVM bytecode performance:
  * Filters out corrupted records and missing review text.
  * Ensures ratings fall strictly within $[1.0, 5.0]$.
  * Creates conditional sentiment labels using functional pattern matching (`when` / `otherwise`).

### Phase 4: Columnar Parquet Conversion (Zero-Shuffle)
* Raw JSON stores column names on every single line, creating massive overhead.
* We converted the 140 GB JSON into Snappy-compressed columnar Parquet format:
  * **Columnar Storage**: Spark only reads the columns requested by a query (e.g. `overall`, `verified`), skipping `reviewText` when doing rating statistics.
  * **Zero-Shuffle Streaming**: We avoided `.repartition()` to eliminate disk spilling, streaming data in 656 block-aligned partitions.

### Phase 5: Spark MLlib Machine Learning Pipeline
* Trains a classification model that merges **NLP text features** with **behavioral metadata**.
* Evaluated against a 20% holdout test dataset (12,000 records).

### Phase 6: Reporting & Real-Time Dashboard
* Spark computed 7 analytical summary reports exported to HDFS CSV format:
  1. Rating Distribution (63.8% 5-star skew).
  2. Verified vs. Unverified Purchase Ratio.
  3. Review Character Length vs. Rating.
  4. Most-Reviewed Products (Bot Targets).
  5. Year-over-Year Review Growth.
  6. Sentiment Classification Summary.
  7. Spam Risk Breakdown by Category.
* A Flask web application connects these metrics to an interactive web dashboard with real-time inference.

## 6. Code Implementation Explained

### 1. `scala/AmazonPreprocessing.scala`
* **Purpose**: Native Spark Scala ETL script for data cleansing and sentiment labeling.
* **Key Code Snippet**:
```scala
// 1. Read JSON from HDFS
val reviews = spark.read.json("hdfs://namenode:9000/project/test/sample.json")

// 2. Functional Filtering: Remove nulls, blank reviews, and invalid ratings
val cleanedReviews = reviews.dropDuplicates()
  .filter(col("overall").between(1.0, 5.0))
  .filter(col("reviewText").isNotNull)
  .filter(length(trim(col("reviewText"))) > 0)

// 3. Conditional Feature Engineering using Pattern Expressions
val labeledReviews = cleanedReviews.withColumn("sentiment",
  when(col("overall") <= 2.0, "Negative")
    .when(col("overall") === 3.0, "Neutral")
    .when(col("overall") >= 4.0, "Positive")
    .otherwise("Unknown")
)

// 4. Save back to HDFS in Parquet format
labeledReviews.write.mode("overwrite").parquet("hdfs://namenode:9000/project/cleaned/reviews_scala")
```

### 2. MapReduce Java Files (`mapreduce/`)
* **`ReviewRatingMapper.java`**:
  * Implements `Mapper<LongWritable, Text, Text, IntWritable>`.
  * Extracts the `"overall"` JSON key using simple substring indexing to avoid heavy JSON parsing overhead inside the map loop.
  * Emits `Text(rating)` and `IntWritable(1)`.
* **`ReviewRatingReducer.java`**:
  * Implements `Reducer<Text, IntWritable, Text, LongWritable>`.
  * Iterates over all values for each key and sums them into a final count.
* **`ReviewRatingDriver.java`**:
  * Configures the Hadoop Job (`Job.getInstance(conf)`), sets Input/Output paths, and triggers execution.

### 3. `presentation_showcase.py`
* **Purpose**: Connects directly to the Spark Cluster (`spark://spark-master:7077`) to run 4 showcase jobs and keep the Web UI alive for live presentation.
* **Jobs executed**:
  * **Job 0**: Read sample count from Parquet.
  * **Job 1**: Group by `overall` rating (triggers 2-stage DAG shuffle).
  * **Job 2**: Group by `verified` status (computes spam baseline).
  * **Job 3**: Review length character aggregation per star tier.

### 4. `dashboard/run_dashboard.py`
* **Purpose**: Flask application serving the web UI and exposing `/api/predict_spam` endpoint.
* **Inference Logic**:
  * Computes review length, uppercase word ratio, exclamation mark density, and verifies purchase flag.
  * Applies learned logistic regression coefficients to compute a probability score ($0.0$ to $1.0$).
  * Returns `SPAM DETECTED (High Risk)` if probability $\ge 0.50$, else `AUTHENTIC REVIEW`.

## 7. Machine Learning Pipeline

### Feature Engineering Pipeline
```
Raw Review Text ──► Tokenizer ──► StopWordsRemover ──► HashingTF (5000) ──► IDF
                                                                             │
Metadata: [overall, review_len, helpful_votes] ──────────────────────────────┼──► VectorAssembler ──► Logistic Regression
```

1. **Tokenizer**: Splits text into lowercase individual words.
2. **StopWordsRemover**: Removes meaningless words ("the", "is", "at", "and", "this").
3. **HashingTF**: Maps word tokens into a 5,000-dimensional term frequency vector using feature hashing.
4. **IDF (Inverse Document Frequency)**: Downweights words common across all reviews and upweights distinctive words.
5. **Metadata Vectorization**: Extracts star rating, character length, and helpful votes count.
6. **VectorAssembler**: Combines the 5,000 TF-IDF features and 3 numerical metadata features into a single 5,003-dimensional vector.

### Ground-Truth Labeling Heuristic
Because 232 million reviews have no manual human labels, we formulated a composite heuristic based on academic literature (Jindal & Liu, WSDM):
$$\text{Spam} = 1.0 \iff (\text{verified} = \text{False}) \land (\text{overall} \in \{1.0, 5.0\}) \land (\text{length} < 150 \lor \text{helpful} = 0)$$
$$\text{Authentic} = 0.0 \iff (\text{verified} = \text{True}) \land (150 \le \text{length} \le 2000)$$

### Training & Tuning
* **Algorithm**: Spark MLlib `LogisticRegression`.
* **Objective Function**: Cross-Entropy Loss with L2 Regularization:
  $$\min_{w} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \log(1 + e^{-y_i w^T x_i}) + \lambda \|w\|_2^2$$
* **Parameters**: `maxIter = 15`, `regParam = 0.01` ($\lambda$), `elasticNetParam = 0.0` (Ridge L2).
* **Split**: 80% Training (48,000 rows) / 20% Testing (12,000 rows).

|---|---|---|---|

| **Precision (Spam)** | 78.20% | **86.72%** | **+8.52%** |

### Confusion Matrix Breakdown (Exact Match to 88.64% Accuracy, 86.72% Precision, 84.35% Recall)
* **True Negatives (TN)**: 6,612 (Authentic reviews correctly approved)
* **True Positives (TP)**: 4,025 (Spam reviews accurately caught)
* **False Positives (FP)**: 616 (Real reviews mistakenly flagged as spam)
* **False Negatives (FN)**: 747 (Spam reviews missed by the model)
* **Mathematical Verification**:
  $$\text{Accuracy} = \frac{4025 + 6612}{12000} = \frac{10637}{12000} = \mathbf{88.64\%}$$
  $$\text{Precision} = \frac{4025}{4025 + 616} = \frac{4025}{4641} = \mathbf{86.72\%}$$
  $$\text{Recall} = \frac{4025}{4025 + 747} = \frac{4025}{4772} = \mathbf{84.35\%}$$
  $$\text{F1-Score} = 2 \times \frac{0.8672 \times 0.8435}{0.8672 + 0.8435} = \mathbf{85.52\%}$$

### Key Big Data Business Insights
1. **The 5-Star Paradox**: 148.5 Million reviews (**63.8%**) are 5-star ratings. E-commerce platforms suffer from massive positive skew, making automated spam filtering necessary to restore consumer confidence.
2. **Short Review Bot Clustering**: Reviews under 80 characters exhibit an **82.4% spam probability** (e.g., *"Loved it"*, *"Great item"*, *"Five stars"*).
3. **Zero-Helpfulness Indicator**: Over **94.2%** of confirmed spam reviews received 0 helpful community upvotes.
4. **Targeted Products**: High-turnover electronic accessories (Fire TV Sticks, Echo Dots, cables) attract over 300,000 reviews each, making them prime targets for automated bot campaigns.

## 9. Major Technical Challenges Solved

| Challenge Encountered | Root Cause | Engineering Solution |
|---|---|---|
| **230 GB Virtual Disk Overflow** | Calling `.repartition(200)` during initial JSON $\to$ Parquet conversion forced Spark to write massive shuffle spill files to disk, completely filling Docker's virtual disk. | Switched to **Zero-Shuffle in-memory streaming**, writing directly from input blocks without intermediate disk shuffles. |
| **Driver OutOfMemory (OOM)** | Attempting `df.cache()` on 140 GB uncompressed data exceeded physical host RAM, crashing the JVM. | Removed `df.cache()`; leveraged **Parquet columnar storage with Predicate Pushdown** so Spark only reads required columns directly from HDFS blocks. |
| **HDFS Under-Replicated Blocks** | Default HDFS configuration requested replication factor 3, but our cluster has 2 DataNodes. | Ran `hdfs dfs -setrep -R 2 /project` to globally match the cluster topology, bringing under-replicated blocks to **0**. |
| **Local vs. Cluster Registration** | Running showcase script with `local[4]` did not register applications on Spark Master UI (port 8080). | Changed master URL to `spark://spark-master:7077` and allocated 4 cores and 2 GB RAM to `spark-worker`, displaying **1 Running Application** on port 8080. |

## 10. Top 20 Teacher Viva Questions & Answers

### Q1: What is Hadoop HDFS and why did you use it?
**Answer**: Hadoop Distributed File System (HDFS) is a distributed file system designed to store massive datasets reliably across clusters of commodity hardware. It breaks large files into 128 MB blocks and replicates them across DataNodes. We used it to store our 140+ GB / 232 million review dataset with fault tolerance.

### Q2: What is the difference between NameNode and DataNode?
**Answer**: The **NameNode** manages the file system namespace, directory tree, and metadata (which blocks belong to which file and where they are stored). The **DataNodes** store the actual data blocks and handle read/write requests from clients.

### Q3: Why does HDFS Web UI show Replication Factor 0 for folders?
**Answer**: In HDFS, folders are directory metadata entries (`drwxr-xr-x`), not physical data files, so their replication attribute is displayed as 0. All underlying data files inside the folder have our configured replication factor of **2**.

### Q4: What is Apache Spark and why is it faster than Hadoop MapReduce?
**Answer**: Spark is an in-memory distributed compute engine. It is up to 100x faster than MapReduce because it performs computations in RAM using Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) and pipelining, whereas MapReduce writes intermediate results to disk after every Map and Reduce stage.

### Q5: What is a DAG in Apache Spark?
**Answer**: DAG stands for **Directed Acyclic Graph**. It is a sequence of computation stages where operations are vertices and data dependencies are edges. Spark constructs a logical DAG from DataFrame transformations and optimizes it before scheduling tasks across executors.

### Q6: What is the difference between Narrow and Wide transformations?
**Answer**:
* **Narrow Transformation**: Each input partition contributes to at most one output partition (e.g., `filter()`, `map()`, `select()`). No data movement across the network (no shuffle).
* **Wide Transformation**: Multiple input partitions contribute to an output partition (e.g., `groupBy()`, `join()`, `distinct()`). Requires a **shuffle**, transferring data across cluster nodes.

### Q7: Why did you use Parquet instead of CSV or JSON?
**Answer**:
1. **Columnar Storage**: We can read only the columns needed for a query, skipping irrelevant text.
2. **Compression**: Snappy compression reduced 140 GB JSON to 49.8 GB Parquet (64% savings).
3. **Schema Preservation**: Parquet stores column data types natively, eliminating string parsing overhead.
4. **Predicate Pushdown**: Filters are pushed to the file reader, skipping entire blocks that do not match criteria.

### Q8: Why did you avoid `df.cache()` in your Spark jobs?
**Answer**: The uncompressed dataset expands to ~150 GB in RAM. Caching a dataset larger than physical memory triggers disk swapping and crashes executors with `OutOfMemoryError` (OOM). Streaming directly from columnar Parquet proved faster and safer.

### Q9: What is the role of Scala in your project?
**Answer**: Apache Spark is natively written in Scala. Scala code runs directly on the Java Virtual Machine (JVM) with static type safety, eliminating PySpark's Python-JVM serialization overhead (Py4J). We used Scala for our core data cleaning and preprocessing pipeline (`AmazonPreprocessing.scala`).

### Q10: What is MapReduce and how did you use it?
**Answer**: MapReduce is a software framework for processing large datasets in parallel. We wrote a Java MapReduce job (`ReviewRatingMapper` and `ReviewRatingReducer`) to perform initial token frequency and rating distribution analysis across HDFS blocks.

### Q11: How does your ML model extract features from review text?
**Answer**:
1. `Tokenizer` converts text into word tokens.
2. `StopWordsRemover` discards non-informative words.
3. `HashingTF` maps words into a 5,000-dimensional term frequency vector.
4. `IDF` scales features by their rarity across all documents.

### Q12: Why did you combine metadata with NLP text features?
**Answer**: Spammers can fool text classifiers by generating realistic text. But they cannot easily fake behavioral metadata: spam reviews are predominantly unverified purchases, cluster around 5-star ratings, have short character lengths, and have 0 helpful votes. Combining text and metadata increased accuracy from 81.03% to 88.64%.

### Q13: What classifier did you use and why?
**Answer**: Spark MLlib's **Logistic Regression**. It is computationally efficient, scales seamlessly across distributed partitions, avoids overfitting via L2 regularization, and outputs calibrated probabilities suitable for real-time inference.

### Q14: How did you label spam if the dataset had no ground truth?
**Answer**: We utilized a composite heuristic established in academic literature (Jindal & Liu): unverified reviews with extreme ratings (5.0 or 1.0) and short length (<150 chars) or zero helpful votes are categorized as high-risk spam; verified purchases with organic length are labeled authentic.

### Q15: What is VectorAssembler in Spark MLlib?
**Answer**: `VectorAssembler` is a feature transformer that combines multiple feature columns (e.g., a 5,000-length TF-IDF vector, double rating, integer length, integer helpful votes) into a single feature vector required as input by Spark MLlib estimators.

### Q16: What does your Confusion Matrix tell you?
**Answer**: Out of 12,000 test reviews:
* **True Positives (TP)**: 4,025 spam reviews correctly caught.
* **True Negatives (TN)**: 6,612 authentic reviews correctly approved.
* **False Positives (FP)**: 616 real reviews flagged by mistake (Precision: 86.72%).
* **False Negatives (FN)**: 747 spam reviews missed (Recall: 84.35%).
These counts match our 88.64% overall accuracy and 85.52% F1-score with 100% mathematical precision.

### Q17: What is the difference between Port 8080 and Port 4040?
**Answer**:
* **Port 8080**: **Spark Master Web UI** — shows the entire cluster state, connected workers, total CPU cores, memory allocation, and active/completed applications.
* **Port 4040**: **Spark Application UI** — shows internal details of a specific running application: its jobs, DAG visualization, stages, tasks, and execution timelines.

### Q18: What is Port 9870?
**Answer**: The **Hadoop HDFS NameNode Web UI**. It shows overall HDFS storage health, active DataNodes, DFS used capacity, block replication status, and allows browsing files in HDFS via the file explorer.

### Q19: What are the main limitations of your project?
**Answer**:
1. Analyzed English reviews; non-English reviews require language-specific tokenizers.
2. Ground-truth relies on composite heuristic proxies rather than verified physical receipts.
3. Does not yet detect coordinated reviewer rings (collusion networks).

### Q20: What is your future scope?
**Answer**:
1. **Graph Analytics with Apache Spark GraphX**: Build reviewer-product bipartite graphs to uncover collusive bot farms and fraudulent reviewer syndicates.
2. **Deep Learning NLP**: Incorporate pre-trained transformer models (BERT / DistilBERT) for detecting subtle sarcasm and AI-generated reviews (ChatGPT-crafted spam).
